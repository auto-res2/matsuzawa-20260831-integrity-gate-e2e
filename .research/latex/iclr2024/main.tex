\begin{abstract}
We revisit the classical observation that linear classifiers cannot
separate XOR-structured data, as an end-to-end exercise of a
verification pipeline in which every reported number is bound to a
recorded execution. Averaged over five random seeds, a one-hidden-layer
perceptron (the proposed model) reaches a test accuracy of
\airasval{acc_ours}~($\pm$\airasval{acc_ours_std}) on synthetic XOR
blobs, while a logistic-regression baseline reaches
\airasval{acc_baseline}~($\pm$\airasval{acc_baseline_std}), a relative
improvement of \airasval{acc_gain_pct}\%. Every value, table, and chart
in this report is generated deterministically from the run outputs and
cross-checked against the execution platform's stored artifacts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
The dataset consists of four Gaussian blobs at the corners of a square,
labeled XOR-style, so the two classes are not linearly separable. Both
models are trained with full-batch gradient descent for
\airasval{epochs} epochs on \airasval{n_train} training points and
evaluated on \airasval{n_test} held-out points; every configuration is
repeated over five random seeds and reported as mean and standard
deviation. The construction follows the textbook XOR argument, first
popularized in \unverified{1969}.
